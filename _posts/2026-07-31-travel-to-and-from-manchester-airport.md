I've been flying a decent amount from Manchester Airport at 7:00 on Saturday and coming back on the Sunday. Here are some notes on my experience.

The trains don't run extremely early on Saturday so I set off the previous night and sleep in the airport. The latest I could manage is probably Garforth at 22:45 -> Leeds at 23:16 -> Manchester Piccadilly at 00:45 -> Manchester Airport at 01:02. There are some later trains at Manchester Piccadilly to the airport, but no way to get there without walking from Manchester Victoria, which I had to do once. It's a pretty easy walk. There are also trams, but I don't know how to use them.

Buying an open ticket on the day runs around £40. Buying a specific train is about half that. Buying several weeks early can come down to £15 or so but you run the risk of missing the train or it being cancelled, so I like to enter the station or come to just before the ticket barriers and then buy the ticket on an app.

You can use an app from any franchisee to buy a ticket for any train (or set of trains). I use Transpennine Express to buy my Transpennine-Northern set. It needs an account; I suspect they all do. You can view the platforms on the "plan journey" screen and unfortunately not from the ticket screen, so you have to awkwardly keep it open or search for the station again. No ads, though.

The Northern trains have plug sockets providing power to charge your phone (slowly). Transpennine have USB slots on some seats, and also have WiFi.

---

Manchester Airport makes it pretty hard to sleep in. The seats have fixed armrests, so you can't lie across them. I sometimes see the ones in the long blue walkway with the armrests tilted up, but I haven't been able to put them up myself, and when I bring them down they lock into position. Screwdriver to loosen the exposed screw, maybe?

The actual homeless people I see tend to sleep above the train station, behind the statues, out of the way. There's less foot traffic there but it's still on the floor.

Ryanair flights depart from Terminal 3, everything else from Terminal 2. Terminal 2 was renovated recently so it's very open and modern, but there's no capsule hotels or anything to make it easier to sleep.

Before security, there's not much comfortable. Some people sleep on the vents but I never managed to get comfortable there. On the corridors at the back by security there are:
* chairs, where you're limited to one by the armrests. Some people manage to contort themselves over several chairs; I could not manage this
* long metal stools. Hard, but you can bring a mat or mattress to soften. Elevated. Fits one person. Probably the best accessible pick
* the curved wooden bench in the corner. Somebody is always on this so I've never managed to try, but might be the best option

I wandered over to Terminal 3 but there didn't seem any better picks than these. The door to the out-of-the-way dark area that was convenient before the renovation is now locked.

You can head through security about 3 hours before the flight, and once you get through there are comfortable leather sofas near the A gates which are nice to sleep on, and also quiet and out of the way. There are also some in the middle by the giant LEGO people, but those are more central and noisier.

You can charge your phone on the wall sockets. There are USB sockets on the benches, but I never got these to work. The ones in the cafes presumably work as well. There is WiFi in theory, but in practice it's supposed to last ~4 hours for free and tends not to last that long.

After you get on the plane you can sleep there, and also there are USB sockets to charge your phone.
